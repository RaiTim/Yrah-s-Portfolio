Weekly Journals

Week 1 to Week 6 · October 6 – November 12, 2026

Week 1 · October 6 – 9, 2026

Week 1

Entering the gates of Gordon Heights National High School during our formal endorsement to the School Principal, administrators, and my Cooperating Teacher, Ma’am Adelyn O. Damasco, felt both surreal and grounding. As I toured the campus— from the English Nook and Speech Laboratory to the bustling classrooms—I was struck by how deeply the school lives out its I-CARE core values (Compassionate, Accommodating, Resilient, Excellent). Observing my first afternoon-to-evening shifts running until 7:15 PM humbled me; I realized right away that teaching here requires extraordinary stamina, patience, and genuine heart.

Week 2 · October 12 – 16, 2026

Week 2

Settling into Ma’am Damasco’s five distinct Grade 8 sections (Jaena – SPJ, Zara – SPTVE, Talaghay, Ulay, and Asimov – Robotics) opened my eyes to how wonderfully diverse learners truly are. As I profiled four individual students and administered our “What Makes a Good Teacher?” survey, reading their honest words—where 90% placed patience, kindness, and respect above everything else—touched me deeply. Assisting during small-group tasks taught me that behind every quiet or restless student is an adolescent simply waiting for a teacher who will meet them with warmth and understanding.

Week 3 · October 19 – 23, 2026

Week 3

Sitting down for mentorship conversations with Ma’am Damasco and our School Principal gave me a much deeper appreciation for the quiet dedication behind every lesson. Observing Ma’am Damasco unpack lessons on diction and style across her Grade 8 sections showed me how deliberately chosen words shape tone, mood, and meaning in everyday communication. Watching how she guided students to compare formal and informal expressions—and patiently encouraged hesitant learners to share their own examples—made me realize that teaching language is really about helping young people find their authentic voice.

Week 4 · October 26 – 30, 2026

Week 4

Stepping more actively into instructional preparation—designing localized slide decks and sentence-building graphic organizers aligned with the MATATAG Curriculum and ILAW framework—made me feel like a true partner in the classroom. Facilitating group activities on types of sentences (from simple to complex sentences) in G8–Talaghay and G8–Ulay during the late afternoon tested my energy and classroom presence, yet seeing tired 6:00 PM learners light up as they combined clauses and crafted their own complex sentences reminded me that thoughtful preparation and joyful pacing can turn late-day fatigue into genuine curiosity.

Week 5 · November 2 – 6, 2026

Week 5

Checking formative exit tickets on claims of fact, value, and policy, applying analytic rubrics, and writing encouraging marginal notes on student papers showed me how powerful compassionate feedback can be. Watching Grade 8 learners debate and distinguish factual statements from value judgments and policy proposals—while assisting Ma’am Damasco with Homeroom Guidance in G8–Jaena and campus journalism (Sunbeam) tasks—showed me how a teacher’s care stretches far beyond grammar rules. I realized that every rubric score and written comment is a quiet conversation with a learner—a chance to say, "I see your effort, and I believe you can grow.”

Week 6 · November 9 – 12, 2026

Week 6

Experiencing team teaching alongside Ma’am Adelyn O. Damasco during my final week— where I co-facilitated and taught lessons on propaganda techniques—was the most unforgettable milestone of my Field Study journey. Standing at the front of the classroom, guiding students as they analyzed real-world advertisements, slogans, and persuasive media clips for bandwagon, testimonial, and emotional appeals, and feeling Ma’am Damasco’s steady support beside me bridged every gap between college theory and live teaching practice. Leaving GHNHS at the end of that week, my nervousness had transformed into quiet conviction: I am no longer just observing from the back row—I am truly ready to step forward and teach with preparation, humility, and heart.

Part V

From Quiet Observation to Purposeful Participation

My six-week Field Study journey at Gordon Heights National High School bridged the distance between textbook educational theory and the living, breathing reality of a public high school classroom. During Field Study 1, sitting quietly in the back rows of Ma’am Adelyn O. Damasco’s afternoon-to-evening Grade 8 classes taught me how to truly see learners—recognizing that behind every restless or hesitant adolescent is a unique learning style, a hidden vocabulary barrier, and a deep need for patience, kindness, and respect. Those early observations became the exact compass that guided my Field Study 2 participation: instead of relying on one-size-fits-all instruction, I learned to craft localized visual scaffolds, facilitate structured group roles that kept tired late-shift learners engaged, and deliver compassionate formative feedback that lifted students up rather than merely grading them.

Ultimately, stepping from back-row observation into active assistantship and team teaching alongside Ma’am Damasco transformed both my teaching philosophy and my professional resolve. I came to GHNHS measuring my readiness by how well I knew English grammar and lesson plan templates, yet I am leaving with a far deeper conviction: great teaching is equal parts disciplined craft and quiet, relentless love. Living out the school’s I-CARE values and mantra—“Empowering minds and character to foster a culture of excellence”—has anchored my growth goals in lifelong reflection, research-driven literacy support, and empathetic classroom leadership, preparing me to step into my upcoming full teaching internship with thorough preparation in my hands and my students always at the center of my heart.
