PPST Evidence and Professional Growth

Part IV

The Seven PPST Domains

Below are my personal takes and cross-references of my Field Study 1 and Field Study 2 journey across the seven PPST domains—documenting not merely the tasks I completed, but the quiet moments of growth that shaped the teacher I am becoming.

Domain 1: Content Knowledge and Pedagogy

Artifact Link

FS2–1 & FS2–2: Five-Class Observation Analysis & Types of Sentences Interactive Presentation Materials

What Happened
Sitting in the back rows of Jaena, Zara, Talaghay, Ulay, and Asimov, I watched Ma’am Adelyn O. Damasco adapt Grade 8 English competencies to meet five distinct learner profiles. Guided by her coaching, I designed an interactive PowerPoint presentation on types of sentences (from simple to complex) and facilitated a classroom activity where students actively analyzed and identified the sentence structures presented on the slides— turning abstract grammar rules into engaging, hands-on practice.
What This Shows
I have outgrown the idea that teaching English means simply presenting grammar rules or reading passages; I now know how to build research-grounded, contextualized bridges that help learners unlock meaning on their own.
What I Will Improve
I want to deepen my craft in higher-order questioning (HOTS)— learning how to ask layered, thought-provoking prompts that guide learners seamlessly from basic vocabulary unlocking to critical text evaluation.

Domain 2: Learning Environment

Artifact Link

FS1–3 & FS2–2: Classroom Management Checklist & Assistantship Log (Late- Afternoon Shifts)

What Happened
Assisting in classes that stretch until 7:15 in the evening taught me that a peaceful, focused room is never born from loud authority—it grows out of predictable structure and human warmth. By assigning clear group roles, using visible countdown timers, and practicing calm proximity control, I helped tired late-shift learners stay anchored while living out GHNHS’s I-CARE core values (Compassionate, Accommodating, Resilient, Excellent).
What This Shows
I can cultivate a safe, respectful, and emotionally steady classroom climate where adolescents feel secure enough to read aloud, ask questions, and risk making mistakes without fear of embarrassment.
What I Will Improve
I aim to sharpen my transition pacing during collaborative group movements so shifting chairs or forming stations takes under a minute, preserving precious instructional time.

Domain 3: Diversity of Learners

Artifact Link

FS1–2: Four Coded Grade 8 Learner Profiles (Learners A–D) & Needs Comparison

What Happened
Getting to know four distinct Grade 8 learners —The Energetic Socializer, The Quiet Perfectionist, The Visual Builder, and The Reluctant Reader— humbled my assumption that a single lesson flow fits an entire class. By weaving Think-Pair-Share, graphic organizers, and differentiated station tasks across SPJ, Robotics, SPTVE, and Regular tracks, I watched once-hesitant students raise their hands with pride.
What This Shows
I have learned to look beneath surface behaviors like “restlessness” or “silence” and design fair, varied entry points tailored to learners’ unique readiness, pacing, and strengths.
What I Will Improve
I will prepare subtle, tiered reading scaffolds ahead of time so struggling readers receive built-in vocabulary and comprehension support naturally without ever feeling singled out in front of peers.

Domain 4: Curriculum and Planning

Artifact Link

FS1–1, FS2–1 & FS2–2: Curriculum Profile, ILAW Lesson Plan Snapshot, & Materials Inventory

What Happened
Under Ma’am Damasco’s patient guidance, I aligned my instructional materials and activity sequences with the MATATAG Curriculum, the ILAW lesson framework, and Phil-IRI / ARAL Reading goals— learning how to keep lessons coherent even when adjusting for late-shift fatigue, shortened Friday schedules, or asynchronous weather advisories.
What This Shows
I can translate national curriculum standards into thoughtful, developmentally sequenced learning plans that remain agile in the face of real public-school schedules and constraints.
What I Will Improve
I want to refine my time-budgeting across each stage of the ILAW framework so closing synthesis and formative exit tickets always receive unhurried, meaningful attention.

Domain 5: Assessment and Reporting

Artifact Link

FS1–6 & FS2–2: Baseline Phil-IRI Diagnostic Analysis, 5-Item Exit Ticket, Analytic Rubric, & Practice Record

What Happened
Studying Grade 8–Jaena’s baseline Phil-IRI data—where 100% of learners decoded words accurately yet none met Grade 8 comprehension standards— opened my eyes to the human story behind diagnostic numbers. Crafting a context-clue exit ticket, scoring outputs fairly with an analytic rubric, and writing encouraging marginal feedback showed me how assessment lifts learners up.
What This Shows
I view assessment not as a cold numerical verdict, but as a compassionate diagnostic compass that reveals exactly where to reteach and shows learners how to grow.
What I Will Improve
I plan to practice rapid post-quiz item analysis so I can pinpoint recurring vocabulary or comprehension misconceptions immediately and address them before moving to the next competency.

Domain 6: Community Linkages and Professional Engagement

Artifact Link

FS1–1, FS1–4 & FS2–2: Communication Flowchart, Principal Interview Reflection, & Homeroom / Sunbeam Assistance

What Happened
Listening to our School Principal speak of leading with humility and visible community presence—and assisting Ma’am Damasco during Homeroom Guidance (G8– Jaena) and campus journalism (Sunbeam) tasks—showed me that a classroom never exists apart from families and the wider barangay.
What This Shows
I uphold professional ethics, child protection protocols, and transparent school-home communication as vital pillars supporting every learner’s holistic well-being.
What I Will Improve
During my upcoming full teaching practicum, I hope to participate more actively in authorized parent-teacher consultations and school-wide literacy initiatives to strengthen my community engagement.

Domain 7: Personal Growth and Professional Development

Artifact Link

FS1–5: “What Makes a Good Teacher?” Student Survey (N=40), Mentorship Conferences, & Growth Timeline

What Happened
When 90% of surveyed Grade 8 students ranked patience, kindness, and respect above every other teacher trait, it stayed with me deeply. Combined with honest pre- and post-observation coaching at the English Nook, those student voices anchored my professional identity in lifelong humility, reflection, and service.
What This Shows
I embrace teaching as a continuous journey of learning, unlearning, and self-reflection— grounded in empathy, resilience, and purposeful classroom action research.
What I Will Improve
I commit to keeping a disciplined weekly teaching reflection journal throughout my practicum to track steady growth in my voice modulation, classroom presence, and questioning craft.
